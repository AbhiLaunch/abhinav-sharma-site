01 / About me

Drawn to flight.
Driven to build.

As a child, I was fascinated by airplanes and helicopters—and how they connected people across vast distances. That curiosity led me into aerospace engineering and eventually to a PhD at the University of Michigan.

My work grew beyond aircraft design. I led research proposals, brought together teams, developed engineering software, and won new customers. Through customer discovery, I also saw how conversations could reveal opportunities that weren’t apparent from the technology alone. I became increasingly interested in the whole undertaking: understanding the need, making the business case, and bringing the right people together to deliver.

That’s the perspective I’m developing further at Berkeley Haas and bringing to my work as a founder. Today, I’m focused on unmanned and autonomous systems, with an emphasis on defense and dual-use innovation.

Designing the aircraft

I led the technical proposal for an approximately $125K NASA SBIR award and researched how to optimize a distributed-electric-propulsion aircraft’s airframe and flight controls together.

05 / University of Michigan · Doctoral research

A moving aircraft.
A moving deck.
One coupled problem.

I developed a simulation of helicopter approach and landing on a moving ship deck, bringing together airwake, deck motion, flight dynamics, controls, landing gear, and ground effect.

Explore the simulation +
I applied the simulation to UH-60A shipboard operations and published the research with Ashwani Padthe and Peretz Friedmann. The work addressed the interaction of the aircraft, the ship’s motion, and the surrounding airflow.
