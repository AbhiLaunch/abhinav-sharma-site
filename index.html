01 / About me

Drawn to flight.
Driven to build.

As a child, I was fascinated by airplanes and helicopters—and how they connected people across vast distances. That curiosity led me into aerospace engineering and eventually to a PhD at the University of Michigan.

My work grew beyond aircraft design. I led research proposals, brought together teams, developed engineering software, and won new customers. Through customer discovery, I also saw how conversations could reveal opportunities that weren’t apparent from the technology alone. I became increasingly interested in the whole undertaking: understanding the need, making the business case, and bringing the right people together to deliver.

That’s the perspective I’m developing further at Berkeley Haas and bringing to my work as a founder. Today, I’m focused on unmanned and autonomous systems, with an emphasis on defense and dual-use innovation.

02 / Selected work · U.S. Army STTR

Designing aircraft that
change shape in flight.

For Class 1–2 UAVs, changing wing shape changes airflow, structural loads, and how the aircraft flies. Our work brought those questions together. Discussions with Army personnel led us to include flight trajectory alongside wing design in Phase II.

Proposal development
Team leadership
Optimization software

I led proposal development end to end, directed a six-person industry–academic team, and developed a Python framework delivered to Army Research Laboratory.

Explore the decisions behind the work +

The flight path changed the research.

Our initial focus was aerodynamics and structures. Continued discussions with Army personnel revealed that flight trajectory also needed to be considered. That insight shaped Phase II: optimizing wing design and how the aircraft flies together.
