Selected projects

Aerospace R&D funded by the Army, Navy, NASA, and industry

U.S. Army STTR · Phase I to Phase II · 2022–2025

Adaptive Aircraft Design and Optimization

I led the technical proposals and a six-person team across Continuum Dynamics and the University of Michigan. The Army STTR grew from a ~$173K Phase I to a ~$1.15M Phase II and produced a Python optimization framework for morphing UAVs, aircraft whose wings change shape in flight. I developed it with Army Research Laboratory researchers and delivered it to ARL for its morphing-aircraft research.

University of Michigan · PhD · Office of Naval Research–supported · 2017–2019

Simulating Helicopter Operations at Sea

Landing a helicopter on a moving ship means contending with deck motion and the ship’s turbulent airwake. I developed a simulation that couples both with flight dynamics, controls, landing-gear dynamics, and ground effect. I applied it to UH-60A shipboard operations and published it in the Journal of Aircraft.

NASA SBIR Phase I · 2021

Designing the Aircraft and the Controller Together

Designers often address flight-control requirements after the airframe is largely fixed. This NASA SBIR Phase I project treated them as part of the design from the start, using multidisciplinary design optimization (MDO) for an aircraft with distributed electric propulsion (DEP). I led the technical proposal.

Continuum Dynamics · Research and commercial

Advanced Air Mobility Modeling and Simulation

Through networking and cold outreach, I won two advanced air mobility startups as clients for aircraft design and performance analyses, ~$100K in new revenue for Continuum Dynamics (clients unnamed). The technical work spans flight mechanics, interactional aerodynamics, controls, and performance.

Current direction

What happens after the technology works

After years developing technology, I got curious about who owns the problem, who pays for it, and why good technology stalls after R&D.

Defense and dual-use technology

My starting point is R&D funded by the Navy and Army. I’m learning how defense capabilities are developed, bought, integrated, and fielded.

Autonomous and complex physical systems

I’ve worked on flight dynamics, controls, and design optimization for adaptive aircraft. I’m interested in where those meet autonomy, software, and real-world deployment.

Technology transition and adoption

I completed NSF I-Corps customer discovery with a team at Berkeley, testing assumptions against real customers before deciding what to build.

Background

From aerospace engineering to Haas

I did my PhD at the University of Michigan on shipboard helicopter operations, supported by the Office of Naval Research. I then spent six years at Continuum Dynamics, an applied aerospace R&D firm. There I led technical proposal development for NASA and DoD programs and advanced an Army STTR from Phase I to Phase II with a team of six. I also won two commercial clients through cold outreach.

Engineering gave me depth on the technical side. Haas is helping with the rest: customer need, product, organization, and adoption.

Elected member, Vertical Flight Society Modeling & Simulation Technical Committee
